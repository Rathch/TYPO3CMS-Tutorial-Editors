Accessibility for editors 

People with disabilities can use a page when they can perceive the content, operate it, and understand it. The W3C Web Accessibility Initiative publishes the guidelines that describe those requirements. You publish the words, images, media, and forms. Several of the failures that show up most often on the web sit in exactly that content.

What the figures say about your part of the work 

Two public scans measure how often pages fail checks that a program can run. A program cannot see every barrier. The W3C states that no tool alone can decide whether a site meets the guidelines. The figures below are the failures the scans could count. They are a floor, and they already show where an editor changes the result.

One million home pages 

In February 2026, WebAIM checked the home pages of the top 1,000,000 sites and published the results in The WebAIM Million. 95.9% of those home pages had at least one automatically detected failure of the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2. The average home page had 56.1 such errors. 96% of all detected errors fell into six types. The same six types have led the list for seven years. WebAIM writes that addressing these few types would significantly improve accessibility across the web. Five of them show up in the content and the theme:

Failure Home pages Where you can change it in TYPO3
Low contrast text 83.9% The colors usually come from the theme. If body text is hard to read, ask your integrator. If you set a text color yourself, that color is yours to correct.
Missing alternative text for images 53.1% You write it, on the file and again for the place where the image is used.
Missing form input labels 51% You write the label when you build the form.
Empty links 46.3% You write the link text. A linked image with no alternative text is an empty link too.
Empty buttons 30.6% Buttons in the theme are the integrator's. A control you add needs a name a reader can follow.

The report gives a closer count for the items you type in:

  • 16.2% of all images had no alternative text. 10.8% of the images that did have alternative text used a file name, a filler such as "image", or the same text as the image beside them. More than one in four images had one of these problems. 45% of the images with no alternative text were links, so the link had no usable name. One in four linked images had no alternative text.
  • 15.2% of home pages had ambiguous link text such as "click here", "more", or "continue". Those pages had 5.3 such links on average.
  • Skipped heading levels, such as a heading of level 2 followed by a heading of level 4, appeared on 41.8% of home pages. 18.1% had more than one heading of level 1.

Home pages that the same study classified as TYPO3 CMS had 38.5 detected errors on average, compared with 56.1 for all home pages. WebAIM notes that a higher or lower count next to a technology cannot always be attributed to that technology. A lower average does not make the content accessible. The alternative text, the headings, and the link text are still written per page.

A second scan of page content 

AudioEye's accessibility statistics report a separate scan of about 400,000 pages across about 15,000 sites. In that scan the average page had 297 accessibility issues, and 2% of the sites passed 70% of the WCAG criteria the scan could test. AudioEye is a vendor. Treat the article as that company's reading of its own scan, and use the WebAIM report when you want a method you can read in full.

On images and links, the AudioEye article reports:

  • 38% of images had missing alternative text, and 60% of images had no alternative text. 93% of domains had at least one page with an inaccessible image.
  • 64% of pages had links that were not clear to people with disabilities, about five such links on a page. 80% of sites had at least one page with an inaccessible link.
  • In AudioEye's 2023 index, cited in the same article, 1 in 4 forms were missing descriptive labels.

The article lists both image figures. It does not define how "missing alternative text" differs from "no alternative text", so this manual keeps them as two reported figures.

Read together, the two scans point at the same editorial work. Alternative text, link text, headings, and form labels are fields you fill in. Contrast of the theme and empty buttons in the template are set elsewhere. Ask your integrator about those.
