Legal requirements 

Accessibility requirements depend on the country, the organization, the services provided and any contractual obligations. Your organization should establish which requirements apply and translate them into an editorial workflow.

This page provides orientation, not legal advice.

WCAG and project requirements 

The Web Content Accessibility Guidelines, or WCAG, describe testable accessibility requirements. They organize these requirements under four principles: content must be perceivable, operable, understandable and robust.

WCAG success criteria have levels A, AA and AAA. Conformance at level AA includes the requirements at both A and AA.

W3C recommends WCAG 2.2 for current accessibility work. However, a newer WCAG version does not automatically become the version required by a particular law or contract. Follow the requirements established for your project. See the WCAG 2.2 Recommendation.

European context 

In the European Union, the Web Accessibility Directive concerns websites and mobile applications of public sector bodies. The European standard EN 301 549 provides technical requirements supporting this framework and includes requirements beyond WCAG.

The European Commission explains the relationship between legislation and technical standards in its guidance on standards and harmonisation.

The European Accessibility Act covers specified products and services, including e-commerce and e-books. It does not make every website subject to the same obligations. Its application must be assessed under the relevant national legislation. See the European Commission's overview.

For other jurisdictions, the WAI laws and policies directory is a starting point. It is not a complete or definitive legal reference.

Requirements and recommendations 

This chapter combines accessibility requirements with practical recommendations.

For example, WCAG requires appropriate text alternatives for non-text content and identifiable link purposes. Recommendations such as putting the main message first or displaying a download's file size support usability, but are not each a separate WCAG requirement.

You do not need to interpret legislation for every editorial decision. Your organization should provide clear requirements and identify who can help with questions. The following guidance supports your work; completing its checks does not establish legal compliance.

How to apply that guidance in the content is covered in Creating accessible content. A first review of a finished page is in Check before publishing.
